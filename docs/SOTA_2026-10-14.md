# Relevant frontier and reproducible comparisons

Checked 14 October 2026. This is a scoped primary-source review, not an exhaustive
leaderboard of all video models. Paper-reported results have not been reproduced here.
Source revisions are recorded in [research_sources.json](research_sources.json).

## What we should attempt to beat

There is no universal world-model SOTA score. Atari return, photorealistic Physics-IQ,
robot-policy success, causal prompt-pair scores, and our numeric-force tabletop task
have different inputs, supervision, data, and metrics. Winning one does not imply winning another.

Our achievable target is a statistically supported improvement on a frozen tabletop
benchmark: numeric versus spatial action conditioning under matched information,
architecture, data, training budget, and sampling. Include a strong external-method
adaptation if feasible. Call it an adaptation on our benchmark, not a reproduction of
the paper's published leaderboard result. Establish the pilot before promising a win.

## Closest work and how it changes the claim

| Method | Verified source evidence | Relevance and comparison status |
|---|---|---|
| **Force Prompting, NeurIPS 2025** | Adapts a pretrained video model to point-force and wind-field controls; released force-conditioned checkpoints | Direct overlap with the click/drag interaction. Force control itself is established. Its CogVideoX/text interface and impulse-like local pushes need explicit mapping to our selected-object, fixed-duration action. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19386), [project](https://force-prompting.github.io/) |
| **PhyCo, CVPR 2026** | Pixel-aligned physical-property maps control a frozen video diffusion backbone; simulation supervision and VLM reward refinement | Closest overlap with our spatial force maps. Map-based physical control is already published, so that alone is not a novelty claim. Its known-property controls differ from our hidden-physics task. [Paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Narayanan_PhyCo_Learning_Controllable_Physical_Priors_for_Generative_Motion_CVPR_2026_paper.html), [project](https://phyco-video.github.io/) |
| **PhysCtrl, NeurIPS 2025** | Diffusion predicts 3D point trajectories conditioned on physical parameters and forces, then guides image-to-video generation | Separating motion and appearance is established. A lightweight state/trajectory predictor is a useful diagnostic alternative, with any analytic renderer clearly identified. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.20358), [project](https://cwchenwang.github.io/physctrl/) |
| **PlaySlot, ICML 2025** | Learns object representations and latent actions from unlabelled video; predicts controllable multiple futures | Object-centric controllable prediction is established. Its learned latent-action interface needs an adapter for numeric forces; an off-the-shelf checkpoint is not an automatically fair baseline. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07600), [project](https://play-slot.github.io/PlaySlot/) |
| **DIAMOND, NeurIPS 2024** | Action/history-conditioned pixel diffusion for autoregressive world modelling; published Atari implementation and data/results | Strong engineering reference for small pixel models and a possible external adaptation. Our single-frame input cannot silently be replaced by multiple real history frames. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12399), [project](https://diamond-wm.github.io/) |
| **Diffusion Forcing, NeurIPS 2024** | Per-token noise levels combine causal prediction and sequence diffusion | Useful for long rollouts, but adds training/sampling complexity. Our short fixed-horizon study does not require it as the first implementation. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01392), [code](https://github.com/buoyancy99/diffusion-forcing) |
| **Physical Simulator In-the-loop Video Generation, CVPR 2026** | Reconstructs a scene, simulates trajectories, and guides video generation with texture-consistency optimization | Simulator-guided generation is a distinct inference setting. Our hidden-physics generator must not receive the test rollout's oracle simulation. [Paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Foo_Physical_Simulator_In-the-Loop_Video_Generation_CVPR_2026_paper.html) |
| **Cosmos 3, 2026 technical report** | Unifies multimodal understanding, generation, and action; NVIDIA reports leading results on specified Physical-AI benchmarks | Representative open foundation frontier, not a matched single-GPU tabletop baseline. Vendor benchmark claims are scoped to its reported suites. [Official project/report](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/cosmos-lab/cosmos3/) |
| **Genie 3, official 2025 announcement** | Interactive generated environments with navigation/action interfaces and limited action/interaction duration | Representative interactive frontier. The official announcement does not establish numeric force-vector control or comparable tabletop supervision. [Official source](https://deepmind.google/blog/genie-3-a-new-frontier-for-world-models/) |

The methods above are not all peers on one benchmark. We have verified public source
availability for the selected code references, not installed/trained their complete stacks.
PhyCo's public repository describes itself as an early inference/evaluation release.
Any borrowed source/checkpoint needs its own provenance and license review before reuse.

## Actual scale evidence, not guessed GPU hours

Force Prompting reports **4 A100 80GB GPUs**, **5,000 training steps**, and about
**one day** for its adaptation. That is not a measured single-GPU reproduction budget.
[Training description](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.19386v1#S3.SS3).

PhyCo's Appendix C reports ControlNet training on **4 H100s**, about **45 GB peak
memory per GPU**, and a later reward stage on **8 H200s**, up to **115 GB VRAM**.
Those are source-reported training configurations, not requirements measured on TC2.
Its paper distinguishes a frozen backbone from the trainable physical-control branches.
[Implementation appendix](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.28169v1#A3).

PhysCtrl's Appendix A reports its base trajectory model on **8 L40 GPUs** for about
**30 hours** and its larger configuration for about **80 hours**. A force-conditioned
model is therefore not automatically a cheap reproduction just because the output is
trajectories. [Implementation appendix](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.20358v1#A1).

DIAMOND's project page distinguishes a **4.4M-parameter Atari model** from the much
larger CS:GO system. That supports evaluating a compact pixel-space model; it does
not provide our training duration. [Official design details](https://diamond-wm.github.io/).

Our local prototype's exact parameter count, memory, precision, and synthetic timing
must be measured separately. TC2 allocation and total usable GPU time remain unknown.

## Hidden physics and counterfactual inputs

CoPhy and Filtered-CoPhy predict an intervention's outcome using a factual trajectory
that helps infer hidden confounders. ComPhy/PCR uses target/reference motion to infer
hidden object properties. These are relevant causal and object-dynamics precedents,
but supplying those observations would change our single-frame information setting.
[CoPhy paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.12000),
[Filtered-CoPhy task](https://filteredcophy.github.io/),
[ComPhy/PCR project](https://physicalconceptreasoner.github.io/).

For our proposed task, independently randomized mass/friction cannot be recovered from
one static image. The intended output is an ensemble over those unknowns. If we later
add an observed interaction, treat property inference as a separate input setting rather
than describe the extra information as an architectural improvement.

Force Prompting's released inference interface uses 720x480 images, a detailed text
prompt, normalized magnitude and angle, and a bottom-left coordinate origin. Its
repository notes collision limitations. Any comparison must record coordinate/sign
conversion, action semantics, output resampling, and a fixed prompt protocol; prompt
engineering must not encode the reference trajectory. This is a transfer baseline,
not a drop-in equal-information ablation.
[Official inference contract](https://github.com/brown-palm/force-prompting).

## Evaluation frontier: cause and response, not appearance alone

**What-If World, May 2026 preprint.** Tests paired prompts changing one physical variable
while preserving the initial scene, with separate adherence/physics/environment/outcome
checks. Its Table 2 reports Grok Imagine at 51.7% and Veo 3.1 at 50.9% on that particular
paired score; these are source-reported scores for nine evaluated models, not a universal
ranking. Text-driven real-world cases are not interchangeable with our numeric-force
synthetic benchmark. [Paper and leaderboard](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.27589v1#S4).

**WorldExam, August 2026 preprint.** Separates visual quality, control adherence, spatial
consistency, and scene reactivity. It motivates checking the unselected object's response
to contact, not merely the pushed object's motion. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02603).

**Physics-IQ / physical-video benchmarks.** PhyCo evaluates against Physics-IQ, and the
WACV 2026 study distinguishes realism from physical understanding. These motivate
physics-specific measurement; they do not validate our simulator or tracker.
[WACV paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2026/html/Motamed_Do_Generative_Video_Models_Understand_Physical_Principles_WACV_2026_paper.html).

Transfer the evaluation principles, not the headline numbers: paired zero/reversed/changed
forces; changed target identity; contact versus no-contact; object-count preservation;
and outcome-distribution coverage for hidden physics. Fix the validation thresholds,
count stationary/tracking failures, and score the actual decoded videos.

## Design from several angles

| Angle | Consequence for Click2Future |
|---|---|
| Information | If mass/friction are independent of appearance and hidden, one initial image cannot identify their realized values. Predict a conditional distribution, not a supposedly exact unique future. |
| Causality | Group all actions and hidden-property variants of one visible scene in one split. Compare changed actions under fixed initial inputs and paired sample seeds. |
| Geometry | Tiny 64px objects may be damaged by a pretrained or strongly downsampling tokenizer. Keep the first pilot in pixels; use reconstruction/contact preservation to gate any autoencoder. |
| Representation | Spatial force fields encode where an action applies. Numeric and spatial baselines must both know the selected object and carry exactly the same force information. |
| Temporal dynamics | Joint short-clip generation avoids repeated autoregressive sampling, but is not a proven fix for physical consistency. Long-horizon diffusion forcing is an optional follow-up. |
| Supervision | A trajectory head can be correct while the generated pixels are wrong. Measure video-derived motion/contact separately and guard against diversity collapse from strong regression losses. |
| Dynamics | Collisions cause legitimate discontinuities. Do not apply a global minimum-jerk/acceleration penalty across contact events or force transitions. |
| Optimization | Large uniform backgrounds can dominate pixel loss. Diagnose foreground preservation first; any reweighting must be shared across compared variants and reported as an additional change. |
| Compute | One measured single-GPU pilot beats an invented GPU-hour budget. Record stable throughput, data IO, validation, full sampling, and resume costs before allocating runs. |
| Scientific claims | Conditioning and physical supervision are established ideas. Our potential contribution is the tested design/comparison and uncertainty evidence, pending results and a fuller related-work assessment. |

These conclusions are engineering inferences from the source evidence and our task,
not empirical results already achieved by this project.

## Baseline tiers and claim boundaries

1. **Required internal comparisons:** CVAE E0; numeric diffusion E2; spatial diffusion E3.
   E1 is contextual and E4 tests supervision after baselines pass the evidence gate.
2. **Stronger reproducible adaptation:** DIAMOND-inspired autoregressive pixel predictor
   on exactly the same training/split/action information. If using a history stack at the
   first step, document initial-frame repetition rather than supply real future frames.
   Treat the continuous-force adapter and short-horizon setup as deviations from the paper.
3. **Optional external inference:** released Force Prompting or PhyCo on a small fixed scene subset,
   only if supported hardware, licenses, action normalization, and interface mapping permit.
   Separate known-physics and hidden-physics settings, pretrained and from-scratch budgets,
   output timing/resolution, and failure cases. This is a domain-transfer comparison.
4. **Diagnostic controls:** static-copy output, a deterministic learned predictor, and an
   explicitly labelled simulator oracle/reference ensemble. The oracle is a ceiling using
   extra hidden information, not a fair learned-model competitor.

A defensible result is "E3 improved a predeclared metric over E2 on held-out scenes under
this protocol" or "our adaptation outperformed this external baseline on our dataset."
Do not announce "we beat Cosmos/Genie/PhyCo SOTA" from an easier, differently conditioned task.
